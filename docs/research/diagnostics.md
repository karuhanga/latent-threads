## Editorial scope

This is a compact exploration of how laboratory findings become a report and inform interpretation. The six areas are laboratory readiness, sample collection, preparation, analysis, reporting and interpretation. They are a reading order, not a clinical protocol. Readiness, maintenance and quality assurance recur throughout testing; the WHO workflow provides background for the collection/analysis/reporting structure. A physician's interpretation is related downstream work, not an assertion that every laboratory report contains a diagnosis.

Blood collection is the selected collection example. Other tests use different samples and staff; microscopy and automated cell counting are alternative examples of analytical work. Some responsibilities can be combined in one job. No organization, country presence, staffing requirement, clinical threshold, collection technique, operating procedure or treatment recommendation is asserted. Geography arrays and organization examples are empty without implying local absence.
